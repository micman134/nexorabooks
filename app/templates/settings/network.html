Staff in another state or another country

The address above works inside your building and nowhere else. Somebody in another city typing it reaches their own network, not yours.

Do not open your router to the internet to solve this. That puts your accounts in front of everybody who scans for open ports, which is everybody. Use one of the two ways below instead.

A private network — what most businesses should do

A tool such as Tailscale, Twingate or ZeroTier joins your staff's computers to this one over the internet as though they were all in your office, with nothing exposed to anybody else.

Check the price before you commit. Several of these have a free tier that is for personal use only — Tailscale's free plan says so in as many words — and the business rate is charged per person per month, which for a small team can come to more than this software costs. If you already run your own server, a self-hosted WireGuard connection does the same job for nothing.

  1. Install it on this computer and sign in.
  2. Install it on each staff computer and sign in with the same account.
  3. It gives this computer a fixed private address. Put that address in “The address used in emails” above, so invitations point at it.
  4. Staff open that address in their browser and sign in as usual.

Your books never leave this computer. Nobody hosts them for you and nobody else can reach them. This computer has to be switched on and running {{ APP_NAME }} whenever somebody wants to work.

Remote desktop — for one person, occasionally

If it is one person now and then, having them take over this computer's screen with Chrome Remote Desktop or AnyDesk is simpler than setting up a network. It does not scale: only one person can use it at a time.

Give every person their own sign-in

Whichever route you take, add each member of staff under Settings › Users with their own account and the role that matches their job. A shared login makes the audit trail worthless, and it is the audit trail that tells you who entered what when something is wrong. Turn on two-factor sign-in for anyone working from outside the building.

Getting it working

  1. Everyone needs their own sign-in. Create a user for each member of staff under Settings › Users, and give them the role that matches their job. Never share one login — the audit trail is only useful when it names a person.
  2. Allow {{ APP_NAME }} through Windows Firewall. The first time it starts, Windows asks whether to allow it on the network. Choose Private networks and allow it. If you missed that prompt, open Windows Defender Firewall › Allow an app, and tick {{ APP_NAME }} for private networks.
  3. Give this computer a fixed address. Ask whoever set up your router to reserve the address above for this machine, so the link doesn't change when it restarts.
  4. Bookmark the address on each staff computer.
This is for a trusted office network. Do not open the port to the internet — there is no protection against attacks from outside your building. If you need access from elsewhere, use a VPN.

If it will not connect

The page will not load on another computer Check the firewall step above. Then check both computers are on the same network — a phone on mobile data or a guest Wi-Fi network will not reach it.
A link we emailed says "can't reach this page" Look at the address in that link. If it starts 127.0.0.1 or localhost, it means "this computer" — so on the recipient's machine it points at their own computer, which is not running {{ APP_NAME }}. Set the address above and send the invitation again.
It worked yesterday, not today The address may have changed. Come back to this page on the main computer to see the current one.
Two people editing the same invoice Whoever saves last wins. Posted documents cannot be edited at all, which prevents most of this.
Everything is slow Check the network, then the size of the database under Settings. Very large files benefit from archiving old years after they are closed.
