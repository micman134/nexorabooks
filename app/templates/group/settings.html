Leave both rates at 1 for any company that already keeps its books in the group's currency. For one that does not, put in how many units of the group's currency one of its units is worth: the average across the period for income and costs, and the rate on the closing date for what it owns and owes. Those two are rarely the same, and the difference between them appears in the group's equity as a translation difference rather than being hidden in another figure.

Companies that trade with each other

If one company in the group sells to another, that sale is not the group's income — the group cannot make a profit out of itself. To take it out, say which customer or supplier in each company is another company in the group.

Get this wrong and real trade with a real customer disappears from the group's revenue, so the matching is only ever suggested. Nothing is applied until you save it below.
