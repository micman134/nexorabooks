Choose the file

CSV, Excel (.xlsx), PDF, or the OFX/QFX file your bank offers as “Quicken” or “Microsoft Money” format. Nothing is saved until you have seen what was read.
Where your bank offers CSV or Excel, use it. A PDF holds no columns — only text at positions on a page — so reading one means working the table out from where things sit. It works, and it is the one you should check line by line. A scanned or photographed statement cannot be read at all.

What to expect

You do not have to tidy the file up or tell it which column is which. It finds the heading row wherever the bank put it, works out the columns from their names, and checks its reading against the running balance.

Then it goes through your books looking for what each line already is — an invoice waiting to be paid, a bill you have entered, a payment somebody recorded on the day. It suggests; you confirm. Nothing is posted until you say so, line by line.

The same file can safely be imported twice. Lines that came in before are recognised and left alone.
